AI note-taking can be lawful, but permission to record is only one part of the assessment. Where participants are located, what the conversation contains, how AI processes it, and who receives the record all matter.

Recording permission, personal-data processing, confidentiality, and record retention are separate questions. An app's local storage, encryption, or open-source license does not answer all of them.

This overview focuses on common US and EU considerations, with official sources checked on October 26, 2026. Have your legal or privacy owner apply the relevant rules to your participants, data, and configuration before deploying a tool for sensitive work.

**TL;DR:**

- **Check recording rules and policy:** Identify the jurisdictions and workplace requirements before capture starts. Clear notice and affirmative agreement are a useful organizational policy, not a complete legal analysis.
- **Map the whole data path:** Audio, transcripts, summaries, model requests, sync, and shared links can reach different recipients.
- **Assess sensitive uses separately:** Healthcare, legal, education, and regulated financial work need the appropriate review and contractual arrangements.
- **Verify controls, not labels:** Local storage, a security report, or a vendor's compliance claim does not establish that your specific use is permitted.

## What are the key legal considerations for AI note-taking?

<h3 id="1-consent-requirements-you-must-comply-with-the-strictest-applicable-state-law"><a className="anchor" href="#1-consent-requirements-you-must-comply-with-the-strictest-applicable-state-law">1. Consent requirements: check the rules that apply to the conversation</a></h3>

In the United States, [18 U.S.C. §2511(2)(d)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/html/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap119-sec2511.htm) contains a federal exception for a participant, or a person with a participant's prior consent, subject to the criminal-or-tortious-purpose limitation. That federal exception does not settle every state, international, employment, or confidentiality requirement.

A fixed list of “all-party states” is not enough to decide an interstate call. The applicable rules can depend on the locations, type of communication, circumstances, and legal issues involved. Resolve those questions for your use case rather than assuming either that one participant's agreement is sufficient or that the strictest law automatically governs every call.

As an operational policy, explain the capture method and intended uses, seek affirmative agreement before starting, and provide a way to participate without recording. A bot appearing in the participant list, a platform announcement, or a bot-free recorder should not be treated as proof that every required permission has been obtained.

### 2. Data processing: understanding where your data goes and how it's used

Separate the recording method from AI processing and storage. A desktop app can capture audio without a meeting bot and still send it to a hosted transcription service. A transcript stored locally can later be sent to a remote summary model, synced, exported, or shared.

Review the chosen plan, settings, and current provider terms for training or service-improvement use, human access, retention, deletion, subprocessors, and processing locations. Do not assume that every cloud tool trains on every meeting, or that a “no training” setting means there is no retention or third-party processing.

Our [Otter privacy guide](/blog/is-otter-ai-safe/) and [Granola privacy guide](/blog/is-granola-ai-safe/) examine concrete product settings and capture paths. They can help with vendor questions; an allegation or a product comparison is not a legal finding about your own meeting.

### 3. Third-party access: what your vendor really does with your data

A contract should match the actual service and data involved. Review the roles, permitted purposes, recipients, security responsibilities, retention, deletion, incident handling, and restrictions on secondary use. A data processing agreement does not make an otherwise unlawful recording lawful.

A Business Associate Agreement is a specific HIPAA arrangement, not a generic requirement for every regulated industry. [HHS explains that a cloud provider creating, receiving, maintaining, or transmitting ePHI on a covered entity's behalf can be a business associate even when it cannot decrypt the data](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/health-information-technology/cloud-computing/index.html). Encryption alone does not remove that relationship or the associated BAA obligations.

Before approval, identify:

- Which services receive audio, transcripts, summaries, and attachments
- Who can access each record, including administrators and support staff
- Whether training, human review, or other secondary uses are permitted
- Where processing and storage occur, including subprocessors
- What deletion covers, including backups, exports, and shared copies
- Who handles incidents, access requests, and required retention

<h3 id="4-privilege-protection-when-ai-recording-breaks-confidentiality"><a className="anchor" href="#4-privilege-protection-when-ai-recording-breaks-confidentiality">4. Privilege protection: review confidential information before using AI</a></h3>

For legal work, [ABA Formal Opinion 512](https://www.americanbar.org/content/dam/aba/administrative/professional_responsibility/ethics-opinions/aba-formal-opinion-512.pdf) discusses competence, confidentiality, communication, and informed-consent obligations when lawyers use generative AI. Assess the particular tool and information instead of assuming a standard subscription or engagement-letter clause is sufficient.

Have responsible counsel evaluate privilege, work product, permitted disclosure, and preservation for the matter. Do not assume that an AI-generated record is automatically unprivileged, that human-written notes are automatically protected, or that running a model locally guarantees privilege. Restrict access and use an approved alternative when the proposed workflow cannot satisfy the matter's confidentiality requirements.

### 5. Compliance standards: navigating industry-specific regulations

#### HIPAA (Healthcare)

For uses subject to HIPAA, assess the covered-entity/business-associate relationship, authorized uses and disclosures, security safeguards, and the complete ePHI workflow. A product's marketing badge or encrypted local database is not enough to establish compliance.

[HHS describes encryption as an addressable Security Rule implementation specification](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/is-the-use-of-encryption-mandatory-in-the-security-rule/index.html). Addressable does not mean it can be ignored: assess whether it is reasonable and appropriate, implement it when that assessment requires it, and document the rationale and appropriate alternative measures when it is not. Do not restate this as a universal end-to-end-encryption mandate.

[HHS does not require private certification of Security Rule compliance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/are-we-required-to-certify-our-organizations-compliance-with-the-standards/index.html). HITRUST validation may be a procurement choice or contractual requirement; it is not a universal HIPAA requirement. HHS also distinguishes its [proposed cybersecurity update from the rule currently in effect](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/hipaa-security-rule-nprm/index.html). Check effective requirements when reviewing a deployment.

#### GDPR (Global/EU)

First assess [whether the GDPR applies to the organization and processing](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/learn-the-basics/data-protection-basics_en). Identifiable voices and meeting transcripts can be personal data, but the rule is not simply “any organization that serves an EU resident.”

The [EDPB identifies six possible lawful bases](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/be-compliant/process-personal-data-lawfully_en). Consent is one, not an automatic requirement for every processing operation. The appropriate basis depends on the purpose and circumstances; employment consent can be problematic where people cannot freely refuse. Special-category information requires additional conditions. Recording-law permission remains a separate question.

Plan for transparency, limited purposes, access controls, retention, and the applicable individual rights. Assess [transfers outside the EEA](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/be-compliant/international-data-transfers_en) separately from the initial processing basis. A meeting participant agreeing to recording does not, by itself, resolve all of those obligations.

#### CCPA/CPRA (California)

Determine whether the business and processing are covered. The [California Attorney General explains notice and consumer rights under the CCPA, as amended by the CPRA](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa), including deletion rights subject to exceptions and limits on certain uses of sensitive personal information.

Audio can contain personal information. It is not automatically sensitive biometric information merely because a voice is recorded: the [California Privacy Protection Agency's explanation](https://cppa.ca.gov/faq.html) identifies biometric information used to identify a consumer as one sensitive category. Assess the actual processing and content, along with the applicable notice, rights, and security obligations. Keep this assessment separate from California recording laws.

<h4 id="sox-financial-services"><a className="anchor" href="#sox-financial-services">SOX and financial-services recordkeeping</a></h4>

Sarbanes-Oxley is not a blanket rule for all financial-services conversations. The [SEC's Section 404 guidance concerns internal control over financial reporting](https://www.sec.gov/oca/controlfaq1004). Separately, [FINRA Rule 4511](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/4511) requires member firms to make and preserve the books and records required by applicable rules.

Have the compliance owner classify the meeting record and determine the retention and preservation obligations. Do not promise automatic deletion where an applicable retention requirement or legal hold requires preservation.

#### FERPA (education)

For a covered educational institution, determine whether the material is an education record and whether disclosure is authorized. FERPA has [exceptions to prior-consent requirements](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/ferpa), including a school-official exception with conditions for outsourced services, direct control, and permitted use and redisclosure. A general-purpose AI subscription does not establish that those conditions are met.

#### SOC 2 (system and organization controls)

[SOC reports provide assurance information about organizational controls](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/resources/landing/system-and-organization-controls-soc-suite-of-services). Examine the report's scope, period, exceptions, and relevant customer responsibilities. A SOC 2 report does not establish recording permission, preserve privilege, or replace the HIPAA/GDPR and contractual assessment for your use.

## AI note-taking legal compliance checklist

Use these questions to structure the review, rather than treating checked boxes as a legal certification:

- Identify participants' locations, the communication type, workplace policy, and required permissions before capture.
- Explain recording/transcription and intended uses; provide a practical non-recorded alternative.
- Map audio, transcripts, summaries, model calls, sync, exports, and shared links to their recipients.
- Document the relevant processing basis and any additional sensitive-data requirements.
- Review provider terms, appropriate agreements, subprocessors, processing locations, and secondary uses.
- Limit access and assess endpoint, transport, storage, backup, and sharing controls.
- Define retention and deletion, including exceptions for required preservation and legal holds.
- Have the relevant legal, privacy, healthcare, education, or financial-compliance owner review sensitive uses.
- Review generated summaries before relying on them or distributing them.
- Reassess the workflow when the tool, plan, provider, configuration, or rules change.
