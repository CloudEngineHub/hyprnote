## Intro

Privacy can be a product design choice. [DuckDuckGo](https://duckduckgo.com/privacy) says it does not save search, chat, or browsing history. [Brave](https://brave.com/privacy/browser/) says it does not collect browsing history, while describing feature-specific processing and analytics. Those policies do not mean the services process no data at all.

[Proton's zero-access encryption](https://proton.me/learn/encryption/types-of-encryption/zero-access) protects covered stored content from being read by the service. That is a different boundary from keeping data off a server entirely. Privacy features need a defined scope.

AI makes those boundaries worth examining again. Sources and Anarlog setup reviewed October 30, 2026.

## Privacy, revisited

An AI workflow can involve recording audio, producing a transcript, generating a summary, storing notes, and sharing the result. Each step can have a different data path.

A hosted transcription or summary request sends the required content to a provider. Whether that provider retains it or uses it for training depends on the service, account settings, and applicable terms. Local processing is another option; it does not follow automatically from local note storage.

The useful question is which steps you want to keep local, and which services you are prepared to use for the others.

## Why local AI works

Local inference can remove a remote model provider from a particular processing step. It is a practical way to reduce data transfers, rather than a guarantee about every feature in an application.

Two distinctions matter:

<h3 id="1-physical-assurance">1. Where inference runs</h3>

A model running on your own computer can process its inputs there. Download the model and prepare the application before relying on offline operation. Check transcription and summarization independently: making one local does not make the other local.

<h3 id="2-no-training-risk">2. What else can send data</h3>

Local inference does not require sending its inputs to a hosted model for that step. But sync, sharing, web search, exports, and other enabled services can still create separate transfers or copies. Review the whole workflow before making a claim that content stays on the device.

Training permissions and retention are also separate questions for any hosted service you enable. Neither a local model nor a no-training setting answers every data-handling question.

## A familiar pattern

A search service can avoid profiling without running on your device. A cloud-storage service can protect encrypted content while still storing it remotely. Those approaches solve different problems.

The same distinction applies to AI. Compare processing location, stored copies, access controls, and optional network features instead of treating “privacy-first” as a single technical property.

## Where Anarlog comes in

At [Anarlog](/), we're building an open-source AI notepad for meetings that keeps your meeting record in local SQLite. You choose your AI stack—managed cloud, bring your own keys, or local models—and can export Markdown when you need it.

Our [offline setup guide](https://docs.anarlog.so/offline) explains the preparation required. Select supported local models separately under Settings → Transcription and Settings → Intelligence, download the required models, and test the setup before disconnecting. Availability depends on your hardware and Anarlog release.

The [Models and providers guide](https://docs.anarlog.so/models-and-providers) explains the hosted alternatives: transcription providers receive the audio needed for transcription, while hosted Intelligence receives the context needed for summaries or chat. Optional sync, sharing, and integrations have separate paths. Chat web search sends a query to a hosted endpoint even when both model selections are local.

Keeping the canonical meeting record locally gives you control over that record. It does not make every optional service offline or establish a self-hosted collaboration guarantee.

## The privacy stack is evolving

Privacy-oriented software can combine local processing, limited collection, encryption, and explicit choices about hosted services. These are complementary tools, not interchangeable guarantees.

The early stack looked like:

- **Search** → DuckDuckGo
- **Browsing** → Brave
- **Communication** → Proton

With the rise of AI, a new layer is forming:

- **Cognition** → Anarlog (and others like it)

For each layer, ask where processing happens, where copies are stored, and who can access them. The product name alone is not an answer.

## Final thoughts

For meeting notes, start with the workflow you need and the data you are willing to send to each service. If you need local processing, configure and verify every relevant step rather than relying on a broad privacy promise.

Local models require compatible hardware, storage, and setup. Test them with representative audio and notes before depending on the result.
